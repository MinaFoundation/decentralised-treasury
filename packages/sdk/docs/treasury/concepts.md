# Treasury Concepts

The Mina decentralized treasury is a way for a community to decide how shared funds are used.

A proposal asks for funding. Voters decide whether the request has enough support. If the proposal passes, the treasury can send funds to the recipient. If it does not pass, it remains part of the public history but it cannot be paid.

## What Users Can Do

Users interact with the treasury through a few visible actions:

- create or review a funding proposal,
- vote on a proposal,
- inspect whether a proposal has enough support,
- pay an approved proposal,
- understand when emergency pause controls affect proposal activity.

## Key Concepts

### Treasury

The treasury is the account that holds shared funds. It releases funds only after a proposal has passed the required steps.

### Proposal

A proposal is a funding request. It explains what should be funded, who should receive the funds, and how much is requested.

### Vote

A vote records a participant's position on a proposal: yay, nay, or abstain.

### Voting Weight

Voting is weighted. In simple terms, voters with more delegated Mina stake behind them carry more weight than voters with less delegated stake.

### Vote Count

The vote count turns collected votes into a result and decides whether the proposal is approved or rejected.

### Payment

Payment is the payout step. It is when an approved proposal sends funds from the treasury to the recipient.

### Whole-Treasury Emergency Pause

A treasury-wide pause is an emergency stop. When it is active, important treasury actions can be blocked until the authorized safety process turns the treasury back on.

### Single-Proposal Pause

A single proposal can also be paused. This affects that proposal only. It does not erase the proposal, its votes, or the rest of the treasury.

## What Users See

Users see proposal status, vote totals, payout progress, and pause state. The important question is always practical: what can happen next, and who needs to take the next action?
